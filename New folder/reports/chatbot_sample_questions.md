## Definitions / about CESC

### 1. What is DCB?

**The DCB statement**

The Demand-Collection-Balance (DCB) statement is the ESCOM's periodic billing and collection position. For each tariff category in each sub-division it shows the number of installations, connected load and consumption, the opening balance (dues brought forward), the demand (amount billed), the collection (amount received) and the closing balance: opening balance + demand - collection.

> **Said aloud:** The Demand-Collection-Balance statement is the ESCOM's periodic billing and collection position. For each tariff category in each sub-division it shows the number of installations, connected load and consumption, the opening balance, the demand, the collection and the closing balance: opening balance + demand - collection.

### 2. What is MNR?

**MNR and DC installations**

MNR (meter not recording) installations are connections whose meter has stopped recording; their consumption is billed on an assessed (estimated) basis until the meter is replaced. The statement also prints a "DC installations" column: DC stands for direct connection (confirmed by CESC). When a meter burns out or is faulty and no replacement of the required capacity is in stock, the engineer connects the premises on a direct connection basis, without a meter. Under KERC rules a new meter is to be installed within 3 days, and the usage in the unmetered gap is billed pro-rata on the previous month's consumption. Up to 2017-18 the statement prints DC and MNR in one combined column; from 2018-19 in two separate columns.

> **Said aloud:** MNR installations are connections whose meter has stopped recording; their consumption is billed on an assessed basis until the meter is replaced. The statement also prints a "DC installations" column: DC stands for direct connection. The full details are on your screen.

### 3. What is Gruha Jyothi?

**Gruha Jyothi**

Gruha Jyothi is a Government of Karnataka scheme, in force from August 2023, under which domestic households (tariff LT-1 Domestic in the current tariff booklet) get free electricity up to a monthly limit (at most 200 units), with the state paying the ESCOM for it. In the DCB statement the Government's payment shows up as collection booked by "adjustment": for domestic LT-1 the adjusted share of collection was 16-31% up to 2022-23, 60% in 2023-24 and 82-84% in 2024-25 and 2025-26. Ask, for example, "subsidy for LT-1 in 2025-26" or "subsidy share trend for LT-1".

> **Said aloud:** Gruha Jyothi is a Government of Karnataka scheme, in force from August 2023, under which domestic households get free electricity up to a monthly limit, with the state paying the ESCOM for it. The full details are on your screen.

### 4. Who regulates CESC?

**Chamundeshwari Electricity Supply Corporation Limited: Regulation**

CESC works under the Electricity Act, 2003. Its retail tariffs (the rates for each tariff category) are fixed by the Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) through yearly tariff orders; CESC applies them when it bills consumers.

> **Said aloud:** CESC works under the Electricity Act, 2003. Its retail tariffs are fixed by the Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission through yearly tariff orders; CESC applies them when it bills consumers.
